## Palette A — "Laboratory bench" (recommended)

Material logic: **elastic tether, paper projectile, glassware impact.** Three
materials that all belong on a chemistry table, none of which belongs to a
cartoon. Two of them — the elastic and the paper — are the same two objects the
screen already draws, so the sound voices the animation instead of decorating it.

| Event | Pick | Level | Length after edit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WEB_STARTED` | dsebeste elastic band, sliced (no thwip take), low-passed ~9kHz | −12 dB | ~180ms |
| `ATOM_COLLECTED` | same source, shorter slice, pitched −3 st | −6 dB | ~120ms |
| `PAPER_THROWN` | Frank1100 paper swipe ×4, round-robin, trimmed to transient | −9 dB | ~320ms |
| `ATOM_COLLISION` | SamsterBirdies ceramic clack | −3 dB | ~260ms |
| bond, +260ms | zembacraftworks Glass Tap, pitched a fixed interval above the clack | 0 dB | ~200ms |

**Frequency separation** (the load-bearing argument): elastic pluck is tonal
low-mid (~200–700Hz fundamental); paper swipe is broadband friction centred
2–8kHz with essentially no fundamental; ceramic clack is a transient with
200–500Hz body; glass tap is a defined partial around 1–3kHz. The only real
overlap is paper swipe against glass tap — and those two never sound
simultaneously, since the bond fires ~590ms after the throw began. Nothing
masks anything.

Contrast that with the obvious-looking alternative of a *paper rustle* for the
web: rustle and swipe occupy the same noise band, so the web and the throw would
smear into one texture and the player would lose the ability to hear which
action they just performed.

**Fatigue budget:** 180 + 120 + 320 + 260 + 200 = **1.08s of audio per hydrogen
cycle**, inside an interaction that runs ~2.5s — about a 40% duty cycle, with
deliberate **silence during the flight**. That silence is the player's only
rest, and the flight is the one moment where nothing needs teaching. Per ethane
(6 hydrogens): ~6.5s of audio total. Only the bond reaches 0 dB, and it is the
only event the player is meant to *want*.

## Palette B — "Instrument panel" (real alternative)

Character: synthetic, quiet, precise. The game reads as a scientific instrument
rather than a table of objects.

| Event | Pick | Level | Length after edit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WEB_STARTED` | Megashot12 Rubber Stretch, clean section, high-passed ~150Hz | −12 dB | ~180ms sustained |
| `ATOM_COLLECTED` | Jummit Soft UI Button Click | −6 dB | 238ms as-is |
| `PAPER_THROWN` | D4XX Quick Swoosh | −9 dB | 190ms as-is |
| `ATOM_COLLISION` | CJspellsfish Low Click 1, trimmed to ~130ms | −3 dB | ~130ms |
| bond, +260ms | Jofae Sci-Fi Click, pitch-mapped to `order` | 0 dB | 286ms as-is |

Every element is synthesised or heavily processed, so there is no uncanny mix of
"real object" and "UI beep". Frequency-wise it is *more* separated than A: the
contact has no highs by design and the bond is nothing but highs. It is quieter,
tighter and ~0.3s shorter per cycle, so it fatigues less — but it is less
tactile, and tactility is on the brief's wanted list.

**Choose A** if the game should feel like objects on a table. **Choose B** if it
should feel like a simulation.

**Do not mix them.** The specific failure is A's ceramic clack resolving into B's
sci-fi bond: a real impact answered by a synthetic tone makes the bond read as a
system notification rather than a physical consequence — inverting the one
lesson the collision exists to teach.

## Attribution obligations if Palette A or B ships

Neither palette requires attribution. The one caveat: **if qubodup's bamboo
swash is used**, credit "qubodup" in a Sounds section — the licence field says
CC0 but the uploader's profile asks for CC BY-style credit, and one line
resolves the ambiguity at no cost.

If anything attribution-required is ever added (the OpenGameArt pack, any CC BY
sound), the project needs a credits screen or a `CREDITS.md` that ships with the
build. Both palettes are deliberately built to avoid that.
